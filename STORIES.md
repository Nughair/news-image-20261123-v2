## Leads (1–3)

### 1. COP31 ends with no deal — the longest summit ever — REPORTED
**UN News / BBC · Antalya.** COP31 ends with no pact on Monday after a
record 44 hours of overtime — the longest climate summit ever, and the
first with no deal since 2000. Presidency chief Kurum concedes defeat
at Sunday's closing plenary; the EU and 130 nations rejected a draft
text with no fossil-fuel language. UN Secretary-General Guterres speaks
on Monday, "deeply disappointed". (UN News published 05:28 UTC Monday;
BBC's Monday coverage confirms the collapse and the 44-hour overtime.)

### 2. NATO in emergency session over Poland — REPORTED
**Reuters / RMF24 · Brussels.** The North Atlantic Council holds an
emergency session in Brussels on Monday over three Russian Kh-101
cruise missiles that fell in Poland on Friday 20 November. Poland asks
for stronger eastern-flank air defences; Secretary-General Rutte says
NATO "will defend every inch" of allied territory; SACEUR Grynkewich
is reviewing the alliance's posture. (The missiles themselves fell on
Friday — the Monday news is the emergency session; Rutte's Saturday
call with the Polish president is background, not the story.)

### 3. Ukraine sinks a Russian warship off Libya — REPORTED
**Reuters / AP · Kyiv.** President Zelenskyy announces on Monday that
seven Ukrainian sea drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury in
Benghazi on Sunday; the cargo ship MV Angara was hit too, and a Ka-27
helicopter destroyed — 2,400 km from the front. The Kremlin on Monday
accuses Ukraine of "terrorist activity" over the strike (see brief 16);
Libya's Tripoli-based government voices "grave concern" about civilian
ports (brief 36); Libya says the Merkury was in Benghazi to refuel
under a deal (brief 28). (The attack was early Sunday — the Monday
news is Zelenskyy's announcement and the reactions.)

## Cards (4–13)

### 4. Hormuz attacks hit a wartime high — REPORTED
**CNN / UKMTO.** 22 attacks in November, 31 in October — the most since
the war began in February. Three tankers were hit over the weekend;
Brent crude is around $92 and Hormuz traffic is down 29%. UKMTO's
threat index for the strait is CRITICAL (refreshed 09:01 UTC Monday);
Iran's navy says two tankers hit mines (04:07 UTC Monday). (The tanker
fires were Friday–Saturday; the Monday news is CNN's analysis and the
UKMTO index update.)

### 5. Gaza now a 'toxic wasteland', Oxfam warns — REPORTED
**Oxfam / CEOBS.** A report published on Monday finds contamination
indicators across over 99% of Gaza and 78% of its land affected; air,
soil and water are unsafe for decades. Oxfam calls the territory a
"toxic wasteland". (The underlying contamination accumulated over the
war; the Monday news is the report's publication.)

### 6. Cikande toll confirmed at 24; ~30 missing — REPORTED
**Reuters · Indonesia.** The candy-factory fire broke out on Saturday
evening; the death toll is confirmed at 24 on Monday as the search
resumes, with around 30 people still missing. DNA identification may
take 2–3 weeks.

### 7. Two states sue SpaceX over Grok deepfakes — REPORTED
**Reuters / WIRED · United States.** Oregon and Virginia file on Monday:
Grok's "spicy mode" turned real photos into sexualised images, including
of children. Researchers counted about 3 million sexualised images in
11 days; penalties run up to $2,500 per violation.

### 8. Russia 'ready to launch nuclear satellite' — CLAIM
**NDR / WDR / Süddeutsche Zeitung.** German media report on Monday that
NATO intelligence concludes Moscow plans to launch Sputnik-S — a
nuclear-armed satellite — this year; the satellite sits near Moscow.
Labelled CLAIM: it is an intelligence assessment reported by media,
not a confirmed launch or a confirmed weapon.

### 9. Denmark takes Russian visa ban to EU — REPORTED
**Reuters · Copenhagen.** Denmark puts an EU-wide ban on tourist visas
for Russians to EU ambassadors on Monday, days after expelling Russia's
defence attaché over the Kyivstoner plot. (The expulsion was Friday —
the Monday news is the EU proposal.)

### 10. Dymer toll reaches 11 as Kyiv region mourns — REPORTED
**AP / Reuters · Ukraine.** The toll reaches 11 on Monday morning; the
Kyiv region holds a day of mourning. A drone hit a five-storey block
on Sunday; a boy was pulled alive from the rubble after 14 hours.

### 11. Markets rebound as Bitcoin retakes $60,000 — REPORTED
**Morningstar / Investopedia.** S&P futures +2.2%, Nasdaq +3% after
Friday's rout; gold around $3,620; Bitcoin $61,300 after a $55,000
weekend low; Brent about $92.86. (Morningstar, 01:27 ET Monday.)

### 12. White House tells agencies: clear Congress first — REPORTED
**NPR / CNN · Washington.** White House counsel Will Scharf tells
agencies on Monday to seek approval before appearing before Congress;
Democrats call it an attack on oversight. (The underlying executive
order, EO 14438, was signed on Friday 20 November — the Monday news
is the counsel's instruction to agencies.)

### 13. Seoul: Lee names first woman spy chief — REPORTED
**Reuters · South Korea.** President Lee Jae Myung's surprise cabinet
reshuffle on Monday makes Kang Kum-sil the first woman to run the
National Intelligence Service. Lim Woong-soon becomes foreign minister;
Hong Ik-pyo takes unification.
